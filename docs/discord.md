# Discord Bot and Communities Directory

Deep reference for the maniabot Discord subsystem and the `/communities` server directory. Other feature models live in `docs/features/` and `docs/packs.md`.

## Discord bot (maniabot)

`backend/src/discord/` is an HTTP-interactions bot (maniabot). Gated by `ENABLE_DISCORD_BOT` (default off), `ENABLE_DISCORD_FEEDS` (default on) and `ENABLE_DISCORD_GATEWAY` (default off), with `DISCORD_*` config in `config.ts`. HTTP: `/api/discord/interactions` (Ed25519 signature-verified, ahead of the public rate gate), `/api/discord/info` (public, cached 60s), `/api/discord/preview` (public, costly bucket), the bridge-only `/api/discord/account*` and `/api/discord/servers*` for the site, and `/api/admin/discord/{status,register-commands,register-emojis,guilds,remove-subscription,playground*}`. Frontend: `/discord`, `/admin/discord` and `/admin/discord-playground`.

Commands: `/profile` (tabs for Skills, Top plays, Recent, Card as buttons that edit the message in place), `/recent`, `/skills`, `/top`, `/maniacard`, `/compare`, `/farm`, `/map` (dan, MSD and skillsets at a rate, with rate buttons), `/challenge` (a random ranked or loved chart near the player's rice or LN dan with the clear bar from `danClearBarFor`, a reroll button and a "Check my plays" button that reads recent plays), `/rankings`, `/link`, `/unlink`, `/setup` (Manage Server: per-channel feed switches and the automatic dan reply) and `/help`; plus two context menus, "Dan estimate" on a message and "Mania profile" on a user. Definitions in `commands.ts`, handlers in `handlers.ts`. Text commands (`prefix.ts`, gateway only): `m!` plus a short name (`r` recent, `t` top, `p` profile, `s` skills, `c` compare, `m` map, `f` farm, `ch` challenge, `card`, `h` help), then the player, or for `m!m` a beatmap and an optional rate (`1.2x`, `+DT`); `7k`-style words set the keymode for skills, farm and challenge. The parser builds the slash command's interaction and runs it through `routeInteraction`, and the reply is posted as a channel reply that pings nobody. One command per person every 2s, at most 8 at once; servers only.

Routing lives in `router.ts`: one function maps an interaction to its immediate response and the deferred follow-up body. The live runtime (`index.ts`) sends the response and PATCHes the follow-up through REST; the playground awaits both. Buttons are stateless: the custom id carries the command and its options (`components.ts`), and a click re-runs the same handler. A reply is private for the always-private commands, for `hidden:true`, or when the caller turned on private replies on the site and passed no `hidden`.

Replies are Components V2 built natively (`v2.ts` builders, `views.ts` and `panels.ts` views, `format.ts` shared formatting): one container, the subject's picture as the section thumbnail, buttons inside the card. Dan emblems come from the site's `/api/og?kind=dan-emblem&keys=&family=&label=` (the site's own per-keymode art from `src/lib/dan-images.ts`, rasterized on a transparent background; keymodes with no art get no emblem). Grade, mod and judgement pills are custom application emojis (`emojis.ts`), built from the in-app SVGs by `scripts/build-discord-emojis.mjs`, uploaded once via the admin "Register emojis" action and referenced as `<:name:id>`; every helper falls back to text when one is missing. The snipe feed post and the owner notices (bug, translation and community-review alerts) are the only classic embeds left (`embeds.ts`), converted to V2 at the REST layer. Prism keymodes (6K-8K) use the site's skillset names (Chordstream, Bracket, Jack, Technical, Delay) and hide the internal slots.

osu! calls: handlers read stored users, scores and beatmaps first, cache what still has to go to osu! (the opt-in `cacheTtlMs` response cache in `osu/client.ts`), and spend at most `DISCORD_OSU_CALLS_PER_MINUTE` (default 12) live calls a minute through `osu-budget.ts`, answering "osu! is busy" past it so the bot never queues ahead of ingest. Screenshot matching runs on the serving read thread, at most 4 at once; automatic dan replies run at most 16 at once; `rest.ts` waits out Discord's rate-limit buckets instead of sending into a 429.

Feeds (`subscriptions.ts`): Top plays (per country), New farm maps and Website changelog are offered; Snipes still delivers to channels that already had it but is not offered while the snipes board is admin-only. A Top plays feed posts every confirmed top play (a play entering the player's stored top 200), narrowed per channel on the `/discord` page by keymodes (`keys_json`, empty = all), a cut to the player's best 50 or 100 of the play's keymode (`keymode_top`) and a pp floor (`min_pp`). A feed also has a scope and a player list (`countries_json`, `players_json`, `players_mode`): its scope is its set of countries, or its one country when the set is empty; mode `only` with players posts just those players from any country, mode `except` posts everyone in scope but them, and an empty list posts everyone in scope. The list holds up to 200 players, picked on `/discord` in a window that browses the tracked players of the chosen countries (`/api/users/roster`). The keymode place is counted from `user_top_scores`: the play is in the stored top 200 and so is every play worth more, so the count is exact. Feed posts are built in `feed-posts.ts` (a top play shows a cached dan only; a new map is rated if needed). Website changelog: the serving process reads the frontend's `/api/changelog` every `DISCORD_CHANGELOG_POLL_MS` (10 min) and posts lines it has not seen (`changelog-feed.ts`, table `discord_changelog_posted`). A line's key is its date plus its place among that day's lines counted from the oldest, so editing a line never reposts it. The first read only records, and more than three new lines at once are recorded without posting.

Settings (`settings.ts`, tables in `db.ts` `migrateDiscordBotSettings`): per Discord user, a default keymode and private replies; per server, a default country and the channels with the automatic dan reply. Links made on the site through Discord OAuth set `discord_user_links.verified`; `/link <username>` links are unverified. The `/discord` page (`src/lib/discord-bot.ts`) links the account, edits the user settings and, with a fresh Discord connection, lists the servers the viewer owns or manages; every server change re-checks that list through Discord, and the backend (`discord/site.ts`) checks the bot is in the server and the channel belongs to it.

Automatic dan reply: with `ENABLE_DISCORD_GATEWAY=true`, the serving process opens a gateway connection (`gateway.ts`, intents GUILDS, GUILD_MESSAGES and MESSAGE_CONTENT, which must be enabled for the application in the developer portal) and answers score posts and beatmap links (`message-refs.ts` reads links in text and other bots' score embeds, with their rate mods) in channels that turned it on, as a reply that pings nobody, once per chart per channel every 10 minutes. Without the gateway, the "Dan estimate" message command does the same on request.

Playground (`/admin/discord-playground`, `discord/playground.ts`, refused when `NODE_ENV=production`): pick a command, fill its options and run it through the real router against the local DB; the reply renders like Discord (`src/components/discord/message/`), buttons and the link modal work, autocomplete calls the real handler, and feed posts can be generated from local data. It flags what Discord would reject (component and text caps, button rules, custom id length) and shows a handler's stack when it throws. With "Re-run on backend reload" on, the page polls the backend's boot id and re-runs its messages after `node --watch` restarts, so editing a view updates the preview.

## Discord server directory (`/communities`)

A directory of osu!mania Discord servers, posted by the people who run them. `backend/src/features/communities.ts` + `http/routes/communities.ts`, table `discord_communities` (migrated in `db.ts`, not `001_initial.sql`), frontend `src/routes/communities.tsx` + `src/lib/communities.ts`.

Two things carry the trust, and neither is the form. The submitter proves through Discord's own OAuth (`identify` + `guilds`, `src/lib/discord-auth-server.ts`) that they own or hold Manage Server on the guild, re-derived server-side on submit rather than trusted from the picker. And the listing's identity - name, icon, banner, member and online counts - comes from Discord's unauthenticated invite endpoint (`backend/src/discord/invites.ts`, standalone so it works with `ENABLE_DISCORD_BOT=false`), which also refuses an invite pointing at a different guild than the one proved. No Discord token is stored: it lives in a 30-minute signed cookie bound to the osu! account that connected it, and is revoked once a listing is in.

Nothing publishes on submit. A row is born `pending` and a moderator approves it at `/communities/review`. An owner's later edit to an approved listing goes live on its own and asks nobody for anything - it raises no flag, reaches no queue and pages no one; a listing rewritten into something else is what the flag button on its page is for, which is why the `edited_since_review` column was dropped. A row arriving in the pending list pings the owner's notice channel (`communityReviewAlertEmbed` through `postOwnerNotice`, the `DISCORD_BUG_REPORT_CHANNEL_ID` channel that also carries bug and translation reports) - a first submit, and a rejected listing its owner edited back into the queue.

Reviewing is deliberately **not** an `/admin` surface. `COMMUNITY_MODERATOR_USER_IDS` in `src/lib/communities-shared.ts` is a hand-kept list (the same shape as `SKIN_KEYMODE_MODERATOR_USER_IDS`), the queue is reached from a button on the directory itself, and the review server functions re-check `canModerateCommunities` rather than `requireTrueAdminAccess` - so trusting somebody with servers implies nothing else on the site. The backend review routes live under `/api/communities/` for the same reason, and carry the moderator's osu! id so every decision is attributable in the logs. A rejected listing that its owner edits goes back to pending. `communities/refresh.ts` re-resolves every approved invite on a 6-hour sweep (started beside retention, since it spends no osu! API budget), refreshing counts and dropping a listing off the directory after `COMMUNITY_INVITE_FAIL_LIMIT` consecutive failures; a Discord outage (`lookup_failed`) deliberately does not count. The owner still sees a hidden listing, with a warning, and one good check restores it.

The form is checked as it is filled in rather than on submit: `POST /api/communities/preview` re-resolves the pasted invite against Discord and answers with the real server (name, icon, banner, counts) or the same refusal code submit would give, so a mismatched or already-listed link is caught before a description is written and an expiring one is warned about. Called with an empty invite and a guild id it tries to find one instead, via `fetchWidgetInviteCode`: an OAuth app cannot create an invite (that is a bot action inside the server, and `identify` + `guilds` grants nothing like it), but a server with its widget on publishes a permanent one at an unauthenticated `widget.json`, so those fill themselves in. What it finds still goes through `resolveDiscordInvite`, so a widget invite is checked exactly like a pasted one; `no_auto_invite` means the field has to ask. It writes nothing, and 200s with `ok:false` for a refusal because "that invite expires" is an answer, not a failed request. The modal renders `CommunityCard` beside the form as a live preview, so the card being posted is the card that will appear.

Tags are owner-typed, up to five, stored as a JSON array in `tags_json` and normalized by `normalizeTags` (lowercase, no quotes or wildcards in the charset, deduped, capped) - which is what lets the tag filter match `like '%"<tag>"%'` without half-matching a longer tag. There is deliberately **no vocabulary**: a fixed list of keymodes and purposes was tried and dropped before the directory opened (a list of Discord servers has no business asking about keys), and those columns were removed. Every filter row - country, language, tags - is built from facets the list query returns (tags via `json_each`), counted over the search but *before* the country/language/tag filters apply, so the page only ever offers values with servers behind them, switching between them always stays possible, and a row hides itself until there is more than one thing to choose between. An expiring invite is deliberately allowed rather than refused: it is a worse listing, not an invalid one, and the refresh sweep already retires a listing whose link stops resolving. The expiry is stored in `invite_expires_at`, warned about in the form, and shown on the owner's own row and on the review card, so it is visible while it can still be fixed. Descriptions run to 1000 characters and keep their line breaks (`cleanPitch`, rendered `whitespace-pre-line`, folded past ~200 chars on the card).

A listing may name **who it is for**: `access_scopes_json` holds country codes and the `R-` region codes from `regions.ts` mixed freely, empty meaning everyone, and `access_hidden` is the owner's second choice - whether someone outside those places sees the listing with no way in, or does not see it at all. Enforcement is in `toCommunitySummary`, not in the UI: a viewer who does not match gets `inviteUrl: null` and no `guildId`, plus a `guildCreatedAt` decoded from the snowflake so the page keeps its date. So a restricted invite is not in the markup, on a hover or in a network tab. The art is kept rather than dropped, because a locked card is meant to show whose server it is: `iconUrl`/`bannerUrl` point at the frontend's `/api/community-image?id=&kind=` for those viewers instead of at Discord's CDN, whose paths carry the guild id (and `widget.json` turns a guild id into an invite for any server with its widget on). That route re-reads the osu!-verified viewer, asks the backend's `GET /api/communities/image-url` (which 404s unless `communityVisibleTo` says this viewer may see the listing at all, so the page and its pictures cannot drift), then fetches the CDN image and pipes back only the bytes: `private, max-age=3600`, content type checked against an allowlist, capped at 4 MiB, and refusing any source URL that is not `cdn.discordapp.com`. The viewer's country comes off the osu!-verified profile and is forwarded over the admin-token bridge; a browser cannot assert it. The hidden kind is filtered in `listCommunities` above the facet cut (so totals and the country facet agree) and 404s on `/get`. Note this filters, it does not enforce: anyone already inside can paste the invite anywhere, and a real wall is Discord-side membership screening.

Anyone browsing can **flag** a listing from its page (`POST /api/communities/report`, table `discord_community_reports`), which is what watches a listing after approval. A report is a message to the moderators: the owner never sees one, no count shows on a card, and `viewerReported` on `/get` tells only the person asking that theirs is standing. A unique index on `(community_id, reporter_user_id)` keeps one person to one report (sending another rewrites theirs and reopens it), and `COMMUNITY_MAX_OPEN_REPORTS_PER_USER` is what stops one account flagging the whole directory. Reports ride along on `/api/communities/queue` as a second list plus a by-listing map, so a flagged listing is on the review page once with its reports under it; any decision on it - including approving, which is how a moderator says "looks fine" - resolves them, and deleting the listing deletes them.

**Open to everyone** since 2026-08-11: the directory and a listing's page are readable signed out, and only posting (osu! login plus a Discord connection), flagging (one per account) and reviewing (`COMMUNITY_MODERATOR_USER_IDS` in `src/lib/communities-shared.ts`, plus a true admin) ask for anything. Reads still go through server functions rather than a direct fetch, and the backend routes stay behind the admin-token bridge: which restricted listings a reader is shown, and whether an invite comes with them, depend on the osu!-verified country, and that has to come off the session rather than out of a querystring. A signed-out read forwards no id and no country, which the backend already treats as a stranger (locked cards, hidden ones absent). If browsing ever costs enough to matter, the remaining step is a public list route with the usual cache headers plus a `fetchCommunitiesListDirect` for the anonymous case only.
