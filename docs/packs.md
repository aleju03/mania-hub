# Card Packs, Arcade, GOAT Poll

Deep reference for the maniacard pack economy, the streak/blitz games, and the temporary GOAT nomination poll. Other feature models live in `docs/features.md`.

## Card packs

`pack-wallets.ts` persists the maniacard "pack" economy behind `/packs`. `pack_wallets` holds per-user economy state (shards, charges, opened packs), and for a signed-in account that state is **server-owned**: charges regenerate by the server's clock (5 cap, one per 20s, mirrored constants in `src/lib/pack-collection.ts`), the draw route spends through `spendPackOpen` (which also banks the 2-shard open reward and the pack count), and every shard grant - recycling, the arcade - goes through `addWalletShards`. A client push can no longer change any of it: `POST /api/pack-wallet/{id}` is now (a) `mode: "merge"`, the one-time first-sync import below, and (b) a compat no-op for pre-refactor tabs, acknowledged with the current rev and never written. The merge (`mergeImportedPackWallet`) folds a browser-local (pre-login) wallet into an account that has never played server-side - no shards banked or spent, no opened packs, no collection rows, no earlier import - and is the only moment a client-authored economy is believed at all: cards must name players the server can vouch for (`users` row or the honorary roster), every distinct card keeps at least one copy, duplicates stop at a 1000-copy budget (100 per card), claimed shards cap at 2000, and the import is logged (`pack_wallet_import`). An account with history keeps it untouched; a second device's never-synced leftovers are superseded, not merged. The wallet blob itself (`rev` guard, `savePackWallet`) remains the storage shape, plus `owner_username`, the durable name fallback for collectors with no `users` row; `pack_collection_cards` holds owned player cards, imported out of legacy card blobs and stripped from the stored payload. An ownership row carries only what belongs to that holding (tier, copies, recycle balance, pull stamps, current pp/rank, and a `skills_id`): the card's identity (name, avatar, country, tier label) is one row per variant in `pack_cards`, and the mint's skills snapshot is interned in `pack_card_skills` (one row per distinct snapshot, referenced by id). Snapshots are frozen per owner and immutable, so sharing a row between collectors who minted the same numbers is safe; identity is first-write-wins so a forged sync cannot repaint a card other collectors hold. `ensurePackCardCatalog` (a boot write in `server.ts`) rebuilds a pre-split database once, preserving every owner's own snapshot. Unreferenced `pack_card_skills` rows are not swept today (~45k rows / ~9MB across the feature's life). Recycling mints shards by tier value, with every copy past the first paying `DUPLICATE_RECYCLE_RATE` (half, floored at one shard); that discount is what keeps packs a purchase rather than an income source. `src/lib/pack-collection.ts` mirrors this table on the frontend, and the SQL forms are generated from the tables rather than hand-written so they cannot drift. Endpoints: `/api/packs/warm`, `/api/packs/cards?ids=`, `/api/pack-wallet/{id}` (GET/POST, 4MB body limit), `/api/pack-collection/{id}` (GET list, GET `?missing=1` for the other side of the completion header - the ordinary-drawable pool minus this collection, paged and searchable, which is the "N missing" list on `/packs`, and carries `goatMissing` plus `goatMissingUserIds` so GOAT variants are added to the red counter and rendered as empty slots even though they remain outside the pool-player ratio - POST recycle, POST `mode: "mint"` to persist a re-minted card's skills snapshot, the only way a client can repair a card collected before skills snapshots existed).

`pack-admin.ts` is the one caller that is allowed to break all of that on purpose: the grant desk behind `/admin/collections`, where the owner hands out shards or mints a card with every field on it typed by hand (tier including a GOAT for someone off the roster, tier label, copies, recycled copies, pp/rank, the skills snapshot, both pull stamps, the serial, and the card's face). The guards above are about a stranger on the other side of the wire, not about the owner, so this path writes the row it is told to write. Two things it still cannot do: it writes no `pack_pull_events` row, so a granted card never appears in the community ticker as a pull, and it books no `pack_game_rewards` row, so it never spends anyone's daily arcade allowance. It also owns the two per-collector pieces of a card's face. `pack_collection_cards.tier_label`: a card can be given its own badge text for one holder (a GOAT that reads something else) without renaming it on every other holder's shelf. Reads coalesce it over the variant's shared label and carry it as `customLabel`, which the card art prints through `labelOverride` - the same slot `HONORARY_PLAYERS[].cardTierLabel` uses for the roster's own cards, and it wins over that. And `pack_collection_cards.motif`: bounded JSON (`src/lib/card-motif.ts`, mirrored in `live-backend/src/features/card-motif.ts`) naming an image, a scale and an opacity, which that holding's card floats in place of whatever its tier would have drifted - the osu! triangle flecks, or the cosmic tiers' starfield. It replaces that layer rather than joining it, in all three renderers at once: the overlay shader that drifts it up the card (`uMotif` in `cardShaders.ts`), the canvas front for the stills (`drawMotifPattern` in `maniacard3d/cardTexture.ts`, which the 3D card skips via `driftingMotif` so a thumbnail's copies are never nailed down under the moving ones), and the `/pull` OG embed (`motifSpriteElements` in `api/og.ts`, which inlines the image server-side so resvg never reaches the network). All three place the copies the same way, on a jittered 3 by 4 grid over the 1000x1400 face: one copy per cell so no part of a card is left bare, each roaming most of its own cell with its own tilt, size and fade so the layer never reads as wallpaper. A thumbnail, a share image and the card on the page therefore scatter alike. A card that floats one gets its own thumbnail address (`motif2:` in `getCardThumbnailRenderSignature`), because the thumbnail pool is keyed on the card rather than on the holder and two owners of the same player at the same snapshot otherwise share one object. The browser loads it through `/api/card-motif`, never from the source: the card is painted into a canvas and read back out for thumbnails, so it needs CORS headers - and that route is not an open proxy, it will only fetch URLs on the allowlist `GET /api/packs/card-motifs` returns, which is exactly the set of images grants have pinned to cards.

`eternal` is awarded server-side and nowhere else. It ranks between `worldClass` and `goat`, recycles for 250, and is unreachable from `cardPower` (`getManiaCardTier` has no rung for it). Two writers exist: this desk (a variant-keyed grant), and the draw route's **one-time completion reward** - a collector whose collection holds every player in the current full draw pool AND every GOAT on the current honorary roster gets their own card at Eternal appended to the next pack they open, as a bonus slot on its own derived key (`<id>:eternal`, beside `:goat` in `packCardKey`). Both halves of the header must read complete: the owned/pool ratio at 100% and the GOAT chip at zero missing. The reward count uses `pack_collection_cards.completion_eligible`: browser-local first-login imports remain real holdings but write zero and cannot prove completion, while a later server deal of that card upgrades it to one. `shouldDealEternalSelfCard` checks those authoritative rows and the durable `pack_eternal_rewards` registry; `mintEternalSelfCardOnce` then races a random claim token into that registry and conditionally writes the Eternal holding and serial in the same transaction. Concurrent opens therefore have exactly one winner, and a failed transaction consumes nothing. The registry survives recycling, so letting the card go never reopens the reward. `claimedTier` still refuses every client claim of Eternal outright; a forged Eternal never becomes 250 shards, while the real awarded row survives the reveal's descriptive mint pass. `pack-pulls.ts` accepts the tier in `VALID_TIERS` (and `NOTABLE_TIERS`) only when the reporter holds their own `:eternal` row. The draw resolves the opener's identity, PP and rank from the stored/fetched profile snapshot, with the verified osu! cookie identity as its name/avatar fallback, so the awarded face and later collection row freeze real numbers instead of zeroes. The reveal gives it a ceremony of its own, sized to how often it happens: `EternalBurst` plus `playEternalFanfare` run about 4.4s on shared beats (wind-up, impact, three-stab climb, held resolve) against the GOAT's 2.5s. The cue is the one sound on the site that plays a **file** rather than a synth patch (`public/audio/packs/eternal-pull.mp3`, ~70 KB, two CC0 Freesound layers rendered into one 4.4s clip whose impact is mixed to land at 0.95s, on the same frame as the burst's flash; provenance and processing in that folder's README). A four second cinematic hit is the one thing WebAudio here could not sell. `prefetchEternalFanfare` fires from `RevealStage` the moment a dealt hand is known to contain the card, so it is decoded well before the flip, and `playEternalFanfare` falls back to the synthesized cue (percussion-led: `playImpactHit` under driven `playChordStack` braaams, which is why `playTone` grew `drive`, a shared tanh `WaveShaperNode`, plus `attack` and `glideFraction`) whenever the file is missing, blocked or undecodable. `eternalSample.test.ts` pins the asset's presence and its length against `ETERNAL_CEREMONY_MS`, since a drift there is only ever seen once per collector. The burst takes a `layer` prop and is mounted twice, `behind` (dim, rays, pillar, halo, shockwaves) under the card and `front` (infalling stars, one short flash, embers) over it, with the rays and halo masked out of the card's footprint. **Reveal-all is the case that matters**: `tileBurstTier` refuses to burn Eternal inside a tiny cascade tile, so the cascade stops on it and hands it a viewport-fixed finale overlay at full card size before resuming. Reduced motion keeps the fanfare and skips the overlay. An Eternal hand is also the one exception to cut-pack damage: `effectivePackDamage` clears the cut as soon as the dealt hand is known, so the pack opens normally, plays the full ceremony and never auto-recycles the irreplaceable card. Wallet numbers go through `setPackWalletEconomy` in `pack-wallets.ts`, so every write to a wallet's economy stays in that one module. See `docs/admin.md` for the page.

An Eternal is one per collector regardless of origin. `migratePackEternalRewards` seeds the claim registry from existing Eternal holdings, including the variant-keyed cards the admin desk granted before the completion system existed; `isPackWalletEternalPending` and the atomic claimant also directly refuse any existing Eternal tier as a defense before that backfill runs. Future admin Eternal grants insert the same registry row in their grant transaction. Later pool growth, falling below 100%, reaching current 100% again, recycling the card, or opening from another tab cannot award another one. The explicit admin removal path is the one reset: deleting the collector's last Eternal also clears the registry in that transaction, so completing the current pool and GOAT roster can restore it on their next pack; removing one while another Eternal variant remains does not reset anything.

`pack-draw.ts` deals signed-in opens server-side (`POST /api/packs/draw`, bridge-gated like the wallet: the frontend's `drawServerPack` server fn in `src/lib/pack-draw.ts` authenticates the osu! cookie and forwards the verified opener). One request is the whole open, purchase included: the backend rolls the dice against the same in-memory pack pool the paged reads serve (the type's slice and uniform odds, duplicate protection read from `pack_collection_cards`, a readiness pass that trades players with no stored score window for ready ones and warms the originals, and the honorary roll - bare ids, hydrated client-side from `src/lib/honorary-players.ts` so a GOAT card's face has one source of truth), then spends the pack's cost out of the server wallet (`PACK_DRAW_TYPES` carries the price; a wallet that cannot pay gets a 409 with its true balance and nothing is minted), writes the dealt hand into `pack_collection_cards` right there (`mintDealtPackCards`, +1 copy per slot, `isNew` per card for the reveal's NEW badge, tier `goat` set by roster membership), and answers with the hand, its card snapshots, and the spent wallet. Because the draw pays, the client fires it at the slash (the purchase moment) rather than the grab, and the shuffle covers the wait. The rows land tierless; the reveal's maniacard pass then labels them through `POST /api/pack-collection/{id}` `mode: "mint"` with a `cards` array (`mintServerPackCollectionCards`), which can describe rows - tier, skills, a GOAT's face numbers - but never add one, so a lost mint costs a stat bar (self-repaired by the collection's re-mint path), never a card. `excludeCardKeys` is still parsed for pre-refactor tabs whose pulls rode the debounced wallet push; new clients send nothing there, since the previous hand is already in rows by the next draw. A per-account 30/min cap guards the card-build cost, since bridge traffic shares one per-IP bucket. The completion reward (previous section) rides this same request as an appended `{ eternal: true }` slot - always the hand's last card, even beside a GOAT hit - which is why the pull/mint per-request caps read 11, not 10 (the largest pack plus that slot). Anonymous wallets keep the browser-local draw and economy in `src/lib/packs.ts` / `src/lib/pack-collection.ts` over the public pool pages: they are never synced and never logged, so there is nothing for the server to protect there. What stays client-reported for signed-in accounts is exactly the descriptive layer: the mint's tier/skills labels (bounded by `isKnownTier` and the GOAT roster guard) and the community pull log.

Cutting the pack open through its middle rather than along the perforation ruins the hand, and that is a real economy path rather than a decoration. `PackStage.tsx` accepts a slash in a second band well below the seam (`DEEP_BAND_TOP`/`DEEP_BAND_BOTTOM`); once the blade has crossed `DEEP_DAMAGE_MIN_COLUMNS` columns there the caption says so out loud, and the tear-off piece is rebuilt at the cut's own depth (`setRipStripFraction` on the scene, so most of the pack flies away instead of the crimp strip). The columns it recorded become a `PackDamage` (`src/lib/pack-damage.ts`: the blade's own path, resampled into the card's coordinates rather than fitted to a straight line, so a sawing cut comes out sawing), which the reveal and the summary draw every face through (`SlicedFace.tsx`: two copies of the same face clipped either side of that path, parted across the blade by a hairline and slid along it by several times as much, then turned slightly against each other). The proportions there are the whole effect and were arrived at the hard way: what reads as a cut is the card's own outline stepping where the two pieces disagree about where its edges are, plus a seam that wedges open towards one edge and closes to nothing at the other. Anything wide enough to see daylight through, or any line drawn along the cut, reads instead as a cable laid over an intact card. A damaged pack deals its whole hand at once with no tier bursts and no chimes, and the summary recycles all of it once the handoff flight has landed, through the same `recycleCopies` path the Recycle all button takes, so a synced collection loses the copies server-side exactly as it would by hand. It waits on that flight, and then on a painted frame (`autoRecycle`), because a tile is hidden for as long as its flight is in the air and React would otherwise un-hide it and grey it in one commit - a transition with no previously painted value to leave does not run, so the hand would snap grey instead of turning. Tiles then turn one at a time, left to right (`RECYCLE_STAGGER_MS`), which the hand-recycle paths get too. Cut tiles are inert while that runs: no spotlight (which would show the card whole), no select, no menu. Nothing is discounted for damage: the cost of the joke is the cards, not the shard rate. The one exception is a hand containing the one-time Eternal reward: once the server's deal arrives, `effectivePackDamage` clears the cut and the entire hand follows the normal reveal/summary path, because silently auto-recycling an irreplaceable completion card would be miserable. Ordinary cut damage rides in the pending-pack record, so a pack resumed after a refresh comes back cut; an Eternal pending hand is normalized back to intact on resume too.

The pack card view (`GET /api/profiles/{user}/cached-snapshot?view=card`, `/api/packs/cards?ids=` for a whole hand) is served by `getCachedPackCardSnapshots` in `player-profiles.ts`, reading `profile_snapshots` (falling back to `users` + `user_top_scores`) straight to what a maniacard renders. It deliberately does not go through `getCachedPlayerProfileSnapshot`, whose presence, projected pp, per-score users and beatmapset covers the card throws away. It does keep `applyPackCardScoreOverlays`: top-play events and tracked plays newer than the stored snapshot, batched for the whole hand with each player's own cutoff, because a stored window is ~12 days old on average and a card minted without it under-rates the player permanently. Both reads run on `ctx.db`, never `serveWriteDb`.

`pack-pulls.ts` is the community layer: `pack_pull_events` is an append-only, client-reported pull log (never economy) powering the `/packs` community-pulls strip, per-card ownership counts and "your card got pulled" stats. Endpoints: `POST /api/packs/pulls`, `GET /api/packs/pulled-by/{id}`, plus public `GET /api/packs/card-stats?keys=` (or `?ids=`), `/api/packs/pulled-stats/{id}`, `/api/packs/recent-pulls`, `/api/packs/pulled-card/{owner}/{card}` (the durable share payload behind `/pull/{owner}/{card}` and its `kind=pull` OG image). `card-stats` and the share payload both mean "in N collections" as *this card's* collectors, counted by card key: a player's ordinary card, their GOAT and each granted card are counted apart. Tier is deliberately not part of it, since it is frozen per pull and one ordinary card sits at two tiers across its holders. `card-stats` takes `keys=` for that; `ids=` is the older form and counts every card of a player together, which is all that can be said when the caller has a player and not a card. Resolving it from the collector instead cannot work: someone holding a player's ordinary card and a granted one has two cards no rule can choose between. The live ticker adds `?pull={eventId}` to that permalink; while the event exists, the payload includes its exact timestamp and new/duplicate flag, so a duplicate clicked seconds ago says when that duplicate happened instead of showing the collection row's older `first_pulled_at`. Plain links, mismatched ids and ids expired by retention fall back to the durable holding and label its date as "First collected". Retention: ordinary events 14d, notable 365d.

**Card keys.** A holding is identified by `card_key`, and every surface that addresses one card - the ownership row's primary key `(owner_user_id, card_key)`, `pack_cards` (the shared face, keyed with the tier), `pack_card_serials` (mint order), `pack_showcase_cards`, the recycle and mint endpoints, `/pull/{owner}/{key}` and its OG image - goes through it. Four forms: `<id>` for the player's ordinary card, `<id>:goat`, `<id>:eternal` for the one completion reward, and `<id>:v<n>` for a card `/admin/collections` handed out. The first three are derived from the tier (`packCardKey`), but accepting a derived Eternal key never lets a browser create its row: only the transactional reward claimant writes one and every client tier claim is refused. A variant number is minted server-side only (`resolvePackCardVariantKey`), which stops a forged wallet inventing a card nobody granted. The wallet import believes a `:v<n>` key only when the collector already holds it, and `applyPackCollectionCardMint` pins a variant row to its key rather than moving it to the one its tier derives. The grant desk resolves a variant before creating a customized card; when an edit customizes a derived ordinary/GOAT holding, it moves the holding, serial and showcase pin to that variant in the same transaction. `ensurePackCardVariantKeys` is now only the one-shot legacy migration for cards granted before those write rules existed; its full-table predicate runs once and is permanently stamped complete.

**Given, not pulled.** A grant mints a serial exactly like a pull does, so nothing in the row said which had happened and every surface went on calling the holder the Nth person to pull a card nobody pulled. `pack_collection_cards.granted_at` is that fact: set only when the grant *creates* the holding and coalesced on conflict, so editing a pulled card cannot turn it into a gift and editing a granted one cannot turn it back. It is stamped with `first_pulled_at` rather than with the clock, so backdating a grant backdates both. Internally, a pulled card that an admin edit moves onto a variant key stores `0`: readers expose it exactly like null, while the final legacy scan can distinguish that known pull from an old variant whose provenance still needs stamping. The same boot pass that moves legacy grants onto variant keys stamps them, in the same scan, along with variant-keyed holdings granted before the column existed; a plain-key grant from before it is unrecoverable and reads as a pull. The spotlight is the reader: `obtained this card on <date>` against `pulled this on <date>, 11th of 198`, attributed to the holder wherever the surface knows one. "Obtained" rather than "was given" on purpose, so the card does not announce that it came from the desk.

The grant desk mints a variant for anything customized - a tier no pack deals (`GRANT_ONLY_TIERS`, the complement of pack-pulls' `VALID_TIERS`), a per-holding badge, background art - reusing the key of an identical grant wherever it is held, so the same card handed to three collectors is one collectible in three collections and any difference is a new one. Passing `cardKey` on the grant edits that holding instead, which is how a badge gets fixed without leaving the old card behind; the collection list on the page sends it from its Edit button. Nothing durable reads an ordinary event past that: the hourly pull cap looks back an hour, the "got pulled" count 7 days, and first-global is answered by `pack_card_serials` (durable) rather than by scanning the log.

`pulled-by` is the named form of the `pulled-stats` count: `getPackCardCollectors` lists who holds one player's card, oldest holding first, folding an owner's ordinary card and GOAT into one entry at the better tier. The list is deliberately not public, only the count is: `fetchServerPackCardCollectors` resolves the card id from the osu! login cookie so you can only list collectors of your own card, and it is **admin-gated while it is prototyped** (`requireAdminAccess` on the server fn, `canUseAdminFeatures` on the `/packs` trigger). `src/components/packs/CardCollectors.tsx` renders it as one wrapped run of names in mint order with a pinned footer for the hovered collector and a name filter past 24. That gate is now the narrower of the two: `/packs/collections` (below) lists every collector and every card they hold, so who owns a card is public in aggregate; what stays admin-only here is the per-card list read from inside `/packs`, and lifting it is a one-line follow-up rather than a decision this page is still waiting on.

`pack_card_serials` is the mint registry: one row per (card key, owner), holding the serial that owner received, where #1 is the first collector anywhere. The number is computed inside the insert (so two mints cannot claim the same serial) and never renumbered, so it survives duplicates, recycling and pull-event retention. Every signed-in holding writer now mints it in the same transaction as the collection row: the server draw, both legacy/first-sync import paths, and the admin grant. A draw-created serial carries `pull_report_pending = 1` until the browser reports the client-computed tier to the community log; the recorder ignores only that owner's pending serial for first-global, then settles it in the event batch, so closing the browser cannot lose the serial and event retention cannot resurrect a later duplicate as first. `backfillPackCardSerials` runs once for holdings that predate this invariant, stamps permanent completion, and never scans the collection again. Serials surface on the `/pull` share payload, on synced collection rows, and in the `POST /api/packs/pulls` response (`mints`).

There is deliberately no `(owner_user_id, minted_at desc)` index on the registry: `minted_at` is only ever inserted, every read plans onto the primary key, `idx_pack_card_serials_card` or the `serial = 1` partial index, and it cost 50.8 MB plus a b-tree insert on every mint. `db.ts` drops it where it still exists. The registry and `pack_collection_cards` are also both pure key-value tables whose primary key is a separate autoindex (65 MB and 58 MB); `WITHOUT ROWID` would delete both and is deliberately not used, because it needs a rebuild of 5.4M rows under the write lock, hands part of the saving back through secondary indexes that would then carry the text `card_key`, and would take `rowid` away from the admin table browser's paging.

## Community collections

`live-backend/src/features/pack-community.ts` reads the same durable projections grouped by collector instead of filtered to one, behind `/packs/collections` (`src/routes/packs_.collections.tsx`). Three tabs on `?tab=` (Showcase, Stats, Collectors; the default is left out of the URL so the plain path is the link people share), plus `?collector=<name|id>`, which overrides them and opens one shelf and is what every name on the page links to. Every collector is public, which is the same visibility the pull ticker and `/pull/{owner}/{card}` already gave one card at a time. Endpoints are public and browser-direct (`publicApi` bucket, `max-age=60`): `GET /api/packs/community/{stats,collectors,collector}` plus `GET /api/packs/community/collection/{userId}`, the last a thin wrapper over `listPackCollectionCards` with the recycle totals stripped, since what a card is worth in shards is the holder's business. Public directory and shelf reads deliberately expose only achievement/rarity orderings: they do not accept recent-collector or newest-card sorting, so the collections page cannot be used as a shortcut for watching another player's activity. The owner-scoped collection read on `/packs` still supports its private Newest preference.

The hub is a cached snapshot rather than an endpoint per board, and every board, the directory and one collector's rank are sorted out of it in JS. The first cut asked SQL each question separately, a query per board each re-grouping `pack_collection_cards` (2.8M rows) with correlated subqueries per collector for the name and the first-find count, and took **28 seconds** against the production snapshot; reading the table a few times and sorting an array of two thousand entries is the same answer for a fraction of the work. Two lifetimes: the collector half refreshes every 2 minutes and the card half every 30, which is far more often than "which players almost nobody holds" changes. Both are stale-while-revalidate. Two numbers come from outside the roll-up: opened packs is `json_extract(payload, '$.openedPacks')` over `pack_wallets` (null on a wallet that predates the banked count, which is not zero and is not printed as one), and first finds is `serial = 1` in `pack_card_serials` (`idx_pack_card_serials_first_finds`, partial on `serial = 1`, so it reads the thousands of first finds rather than every serial ever handed out).

**Building the snapshot was the expensive part, and it is not built the way it used to be.** The claim that these reads did not block the event loop was wrong: local libsql runs every query synchronously on the calling thread, so the two group-bys behind the snapshot stopped the entire backend for as long as they ran. On 2026-08-18 that surfaced as a **25.2s** load of `?tab=stats` three minutes after a deploy (`slow_http_request`, `duration_ms=25164`), and the every-2-minute refresh behind it kept freezing the process afterwards: **6.2s** and **15.4s** stalls two minutes apart against a 0.65s baseline, with an unrelated `/api/osu/v2` request dragged to 5.8s inside the same window. Three changes answer it:

- **The counts are maintained, not regrouped.** `pack-community-rollups.ts` keeps `pack_community_owner_stats` (one row per collector), `pack_community_owner_tier_stats` (copies per tier per collector, so the site-wide tier table is a group-by over ~11k rows) and `pack_community_card_stats` (one row per carded player). Three `after insert/update/delete` triggers on `pack_collection_cards` record the collector and the card a write touched in `pack_community_dirty_{owners,cards}`, and the reconciler recomputes only those, owner-scoped and card-scoped, inside one write transaction per batch so it can never delete a dirty mark it did not act on. A batch is sized by how long it takes rather than by how many rows it carries (250 of the lightest collectors recompute in 9ms and the 250 heaviest in 1.68s, so a fixed row count bounds nothing), targeting 250ms, and each batch is followed by a pause as long as itself: SQLite does not queue writers fairly, and back-to-back batches starved a competing writer for 1.53s where the paced ones hold it up for one batch, 229ms. Reconciles are also chained rather than run concurrently, since a refresh tick asks for both snapshots at once and each brings the roll-up level first: without that, a cold boot started two full rebuilds of the same tables. Against the production snapshot: the scans were 2.7s + 3.6s, the roll-up reads are 100ms + 184ms, an idle reconcile is **1ms** and one pull's worth of dirty rows is **7ms**. The first build costs one 6.1s rebuild. The tables are a cache with a durable home - every column is derivable from `pack_collection_cards` - so a generation mismatch (the honorary roster is a code constant and is part of the generation), a missing trigger, or a table nothing initialized all just send the page back to the scans, which are kept as the fallback and expressed with the same SQL the reconciler stores from.
- **Builds run on a worker thread.** `pack-community-thread.ts` / `-thread-worker.ts` mirror the maps snapshot thread: one thread per database file, its own libsql connection, revived on failure with a cooldown, and never an inline fallback once it has been online (re-running the build on the loop is the stall it exists to prevent). Measured against the production snapshot, an ~11s rebuild costs the serving loop **4ms** of blocking. The thread is also the one place that writes the roll-up tables, which keeps those writes off both the serving connection and the ingest process. It handles one build at a time, since both snapshots are asked for on the same tick and its connection runs every query synchronously anyway. It is disabled under `PACK_COMMUNITY_THREAD=0`, for a non-file database, and in source-mode dev (`tsx` worker threads do not remap `.js` imports back to `.ts`), where the build falls back inline and reconciles inline with it.
- **The header's totals are on their own clock.** Every number the four big stats print is a sum or a count over the maintained tables (measured together at under 5ms: opened packs is the `pack_wallets` sum, the rest is `pack_community_{owner,owner_tier,card}_stats`), where the boards under them need the whole sorted snapshot. So `buildPackCommunityTotals` is a third cached read on a 20-second lifetime, built on the same thread, and `computedAt` on the response is *its* clock. That is what the page counts live pulls from: taking the oldest of the two snapshots instead let the half-hourly card half pin it, and every pull already folded into a refreshed collector snapshot got counted a second time by whoever still had it buffered. `GET /api/packs/community/stats` caches for 15 seconds rather than 60 for the same reason. `totals` is null where the roll-up is not usable, and the page then keeps whatever the cached snapshot computed, because the only other way to answer is the scans.
- **The last good snapshot is written next to the database.** `pack-community-{collector,card}-v1.json.gz` beside the DB file (117 KB and under 1 KB in production), written aside and renamed. A restarted process serves it in ~10ms instead of making its first visitor wait out a cold build, and inside the 2-minute lifetime it is simply the answer and nothing rebuilds. The version is in the filename, so a shape change misses its cache rather than parsing an old file into the new type.

A timer keeps both halves warm 20s after boot and every 2 minutes after, so the cost of the page no longer depends on whether anyone is looking at it. `/api/admin/status` carries `packCommunityThread` and `packCommunitySnapshots` (source, age, last build, last error).

The per-collector pool ratio is asked backwards on purpose. Asking "how many of your cards are in the pool" means an `IN` over the pool's 12k ids and took 4.2s; almost every carded player is still pullable, so the complement is ~1.4k ids and the inverted question takes 50ms. The off-pool id list is read off `pack_cards` (one row per variant, thousands) rather than off the ownership table, and the ratio is `distinct players owned - off-pool owned - in-pool players held only as a variant`. That last term keeps an Eternal or admin-granted variant from satisfying the player's ordinary card slot; only the plain `<id>` key does. Reading it from the catalog can only over-list, naming a player whose last copy was recycled, and an id nobody holds matches nothing. It is counted in distinct ordinary players rather than holdings; honorary roster members are removed even when still ranked because ordinary deals skip them and the GOAT completion count owns those variants. The board degrades to empty rather than to a wrong ratio when no pool can be built. GOAT completion counts roster members only: `/admin/collections` can mint a GOAT for a player off the roster, and before that restriction the board read `25/24`.

A collector's name comes from `users`, then `pack_wallets.owner_username`, the same fallback `getPackCardCollectors` uses, so a collector outside every tracked roster is still named and still reachable by that name. "Hardest to find" is counted in `pack_card_serials` (how many collectors have ever held the card) rather than in current owners, since a serial is never given back and recycling would otherwise make a common card look rare.

The Showcase tab is the front of the page: up to `PACK_SHOWCASE_MAX_CARDS` (5) cards a collector picked, drawn large, with your own row on top carrying its unfilled slots because the point is that you can put something on it. `pack_showcase_cards` is the storage, `GET /api/packs/community/showcases` the public wall, `GET /api/packs/community/showcase?userId=` one collector's cards on their own, and the owner-scoped `POST /api/pack-collection/{id}/showcase` the write.

The wall under your row is a gallery, not a directory: `listPackShowcaseWall` pages over *cards*, one tile each, most recently chosen first, and the tiles carry no name, avatar or heading. Whose card it is comes out of opening one, where the spotlight prints "Showcased by" beside the serial and the collection count. It was a list of collectors with their five cards under their name, which made the person the headline and the card the footnote, gave whoever last touched their shelf the entire top of the page, and buried everyone else in an order nobody chose. That ordering is why `setPackShowcase` keeps the stamp each card went up with instead of restamping the shelf: the sort has to mean "when this card was chosen", or reordering your five would refloat all five and dropping one would drag the other four to the front with it. A pin whose card has since been fully recycled is left out of the count as well as out of the page, otherwise the last page comes back short. None of the three touch the cached roll-up, which is the point: this is the default tab, and a cold build of the roll-up is still seconds of work even now that it is maintained rather than scanned. The wall groups only the owners of the tiles it is about to print (`readShowcaseCollectors`), so it answers in ~30ms cold against the production snapshot, and one collector's own row in ~1ms. Ordering by the stamp is an unindexed sort of `pack_showcase_cards`, which is fine and stays fine: the table holds at most five rows per collector, so it is thousands of rows even with every collector on the site taking part. That is also why the viewer's own row does not read the collector *profile*: that one carries board ranks, which need the roll-up. It first cost two requests in series, the owner-scoped server function for the keys and then the whole wall to find one row in it, both behind a snapshot build. `ShowcasePicker` chooses cards through the *public* paged collection read of your own id rather than the owner-scoped one, so search and rarity order come for free and there is one fetch path.

This replaced an earlier showcase that pinned cards from the collection card menu onto a wooden shelf on the player profile (`ShowcaseShelf.tsx`), admin-gated the whole time it existed and never used: the table was empty when it was removed. Only the table and `getPackShowcase`/`setPackShowcase` survived it.

The tiles are the collection panel's own: `CollectionCardTile` and the cheapest-source-first thumbnail loop moved out of `CollectionPanel.tsx` into `src/components/packs/CardTile.tsx` and `useCardThumbnails.ts`, so every surface draws a card the same way and none re-renders one another already put in R2. A visitor's tiles pass `canBackfill={false}`: repairing a missing mint costs an osu! read and is the holder's business. Nothing on the page fades a list while the next page loads: an opacity transition over a tall list leaves a compositing seam across one of the rows in Chrome, which reads as a white line flashing through the table, so rows are swapped or replaced by skeletons of the same height instead.

## Streak game (casual)

The solo game lives inside `/packs` at `?view=streak` (`src/components/packs/StreakGame.tsx`; `/streak` only redirects there, since old links are out in the world). It is hosted rather than standalone so the pull ticker, charge dots and shard count stay on screen; the collection panel hides while it is open. Rounds ask about play count or ranked score (`STREAK_METRICS`, rotated per round), not pp: those are wide-range numbers printed nowhere on a card and decoupled from rank, which is why both cards show rank and pp on purpose. Right answer deals a new card, wrong answer ends the run, and "cash out" banks the streak (before it existed, leaving mid-run dropped everything, since the claim only fired on a wrong guess). The draw is pure and tested in `src/lib/streak-game.ts`: three pool depths via `streakPoolDepth` (`top500` = 500, the default; `top` = `STREAK_POOL_PLAYERS` 1000, kept under that name so best streaks stored before the third depth existed still mean what they meant; `anyone` = the whole snapshot) of tracked global rankings, loaded a page at a time, nobody repeated within a run, players missing the metric skipped, dead-even pairs given to the player. Hard mode (`StreakPool` `"anyone"`, locked mid-streak) aims at a uniformly random pool position and fetches the page holding it (`streakRankPage`/`streakPageSlice`), because drawing from loaded pages would favour the top; both modes share loaded pages, with `streakPoolEntries` keeping deep pages out of the top-1000 game. Best streaks live in `localStorage` per pool (`mania-hub-streak-best-v1`, `mania-hub-streak-best-anyone-v1`, `mania-hub-streak-best-top500-v1`). It spends no osu! API budget: card art comes from `fetchCachedPackPlayerScores` only, and an unknown player plays as an avatar tile and is warmed in the background.

## Blitz (server-dealt streak)

Blitz is the same game dealt by the backend (`live-backend/src/features/pack-streak.ts`, table `pack_streak_runs`), because a leaderboard cannot be fed by a client-reported streak. The server picks both players and the question, keeps the face-down card's number in `round_json`, and counts a guess only if it arrived before `deadline_at` (`STREAK_ROUND_MS` 12s, plus `STREAK_ROUND_GRACE_MS` 1.5s for the wire; a follow-up round is dealt with `STREAK_REVEAL_HOLD_MS` added so the reveal animation does not eat the clock). Run endpoints are server-to-server (`POST /api/packs/games/streak/{start,guess,cashout}`, wrapped by `src/lib/streak-blitz.ts`); the board is public (`GET /api/packs/games/streak/board?pool=&me=`, `StreakLeaderboard.tsx`, top 10 plus the viewer's own rank, one board per pool (three), ties broken by who got there first). It reads `pack_streak_bests` (one row per account per pool, upserted by `endRun` only when a run beats that account's own record) rather than grouping over the run log, which is what lets `pack_streak_runs` stay transient: ended runs drop `seen_json` and are pruned after `BLITZ_STREAK_RUN_LOG_RETENTION_DAYS` (45), keeping `guess_ms_json` until then. The records themselves are durable and never pruned; the only thing that takes one off the board is a true admin removing it, done from the board itself (a per-row control in `StreakLeaderboard.tsx` that only renders for `auth.isAdmin`, calling `removeLiveStreakBest` -> `POST /api/admin/packs/streak/remove`, which deletes the record plus that account's ended runs in the pool and leaves a live run alone). Removal is deliberately not a ban: the account can set a new record immediately, and nothing remembers the old one. There is no admin page for this on purpose, since the board is the place it is read. `sweepAbandonedStreakRuns` runs hourly from `retention.ts` and closes (and pays) runs more than 10 minutes past their deadline, since a run only reaches the board by ending. Starting a run closes and pays any run the account left hanging, so an account has one live run and abandoning is never worth more than stopping; blitz payouts happen inside `endRun` off the server's streak, so the client never claims one. Requires a sign-in (a board is a list of accounts). What the clock buys is the end of alt-tabbing to a profile mid-round, not unscriptability: the rankings and per-player numbers are public API on the same backend, which is what `guess_ms_json` (per-guess response times) is kept for.

## Arcade payouts

`pack-games.ts` is the arcade's till, shared by both modes of the streak game. A finished casual run is claimed via `POST /api/packs/games/streak` (`streakShardReward` = `STREAK_SHARDS_PER_CORRECT` (8) per correct guess plus a growing bonus at every fifth in a row, so one long run pays more than two short ones). Every payout goes through `grantPackGameShards`, which trims it to what is left of `GAME_SHARD_DAILY_CAP` for the day (UTC), books it in `pack_game_rewards` and adds it via the same `addWalletShards` recycling uses. The cap is the security model for the client-scored mode: a casual streak is scored in the browser off public data, so no amount of server-side state makes it unfarmable; the cap is set below what idling on `/packs` already earns uncapped (a charge every 30s, 2 shards an open, ~240/hour). Blitz runs are the exception and are paid from the server's own count (see `pack-streak.ts`), though they draw on the same daily cap and ledger. `POST /api/packs/games/allowance` is the read the streak page uses for what is left today.

## GOAT nomination poll (temporary)

`live-backend/src/features/goat-poll.ts` backs a time-boxed community poll on `/packs` for who should join the honorary GOAT roster (`src/lib/honorary-players.ts`). Signed-in users nominate a player and vote nominees up or down; net score (`up - down`) is public and is what the board sorts on, so the ranking is the community's and no hide/reject decision sits with one person. When the window closes the owner reads the board and edits `HONORARY_PLAYERS` by hand - nothing here promotes anyone by code.

Banned and deleted accounts are first-class nominees, since a third of the existing roster is exactly that and osu!'s `/search` cannot return a restricted user. Those come in through a manual path that demands a `web.archive.org` snapshot of the osu! profile; `normalizeArchiveProof` validates it and pulls the osu! user id out of the archived URL, which is what stops two spellings of a deleted player's name opening two rows and splitting the vote. Dedupe is two unique indexes on `goat_poll_nominees`: `(poll_id, name_key)` always, plus a partial `(poll_id, osu_user_id)` where an id is known.

Neither nominating nor voting is capped. These endpoints take the admin-token bridge and so bypass the per-IP abuse guard, which leaves the ceilings structural rather than numeric: an osu! login behind every write, the unique indexes that keep one player to one row, the `(poll_id, nominee_id, voter_user_id)` primary key on `goat_poll_votes` that makes one voice per account per nominee, and admin removal (`POST /api/admin/goat-poll/remove`) for a board that grows junk. A vote cap never limited write volume anyway (clearing and re-voting is unbounded), and a 3-nomination cap had people out of nominations within the first hour of the first poll. A vote is `+1`/`-1`; clearing deletes the row. Nominating casts the nominator's own upvote, so a new row opens at net 1. Every write publishes a country-less `goat_poll` SSE event carrying the one changed row (or `removedId`), so open boards patch themselves instead of waiting on their 20s poll. Alt accounts are the residual hole and are accepted: the result is read by eye, not taken mechanically.

The whole switch is the `GOAT_POLL` constant at the top of `features/goat-poll.ts` - `enabled`, `id`, `opensAt`, `closesAt`. Deliberately code, not env: a poll's dates are a product decision rather than a secret or a per-deployment setting, so starting, stopping or rerunning one is a push from this repo instead of an SSH session editing `.env` on the VPS, and git history records when each poll ran. `enabled: false` retires it (every route 404s and the widget hides); `adminOnly: true` keeps it running but unreleased - the board read requires the bridge token (so admins reach it through `fetchGoatPollBoardAsAdmin` in `src/lib/goat-poll.ts` rather than browser-direct) and writes are refused unless the frontend forwards `viewerIsAdmin`, read off the same verified login cookie that supplies `userId`. The refusal is the retired-poll 404, not a 401, so an unreleased poll is indistinguishable from one that does not exist. The shared token cannot carry this on its own: every signed-in user's bridged calls have it. `id` scopes the rows so a rerun starts clean while the old board stays queryable; the window is the only source of truth for the deadline (the browser renders whatever `GET /api/goat-poll` returns and every write re-checks it server-side), and `opensAt` is what the progress pie fills against so it is correct for a poll of any length. Both dates must be written as explicit UTC instants (`YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ`): `Date.parse` would also accept a Z-less or space-separated string and read it as *local* time on whichever machine parsed it, so one committed config would mean different moments on the VPS and on a laptop. An unparseable, inverted, timezone-less or impossible date (V8 rolls `02-31` into March rather than rejecting it, so the parse is round-tripped) switches the poll off rather than opening one that never closes. The board also carries `serverNow`, the backend's clock at the moment it answered: the widget counts down against `Date.now() + (serverNow - receivedAt)` instead of its own clock, so a viewer whose machine is set to the wrong day still sees the deadline that their vote will actually be checked against. There is deliberately no second switch on the frontend. Both tables are excluded from retention: the board is the record.

Endpoints: public `GET /api/goat-poll` (board, browser-direct - it refreshes on a timer in every open packs tab and carries no per-viewer data), plus `LIVE_ADMIN_TOKEN`-bridged `GET /api/goat-poll/mine?userId=`, `POST /api/goat-poll/vote`, `POST /api/goat-poll/nominate` (wrapped by `src/lib/goat-poll.ts`, which also rejects anyone already on the honorary roster), and two true-admin-only routes under `/api/admin/goat-poll/`: `remove` and `voters`. Writes publish a `goat_poll` SSE event (see above), so `LIVE_EVENT_NAMES` in `src/lib/live-backend.ts` must carry that name or follower tabs miss it. A retired poll 404s the board read, which the frontend distinguishes from a network failure (`GOAT_POLL_OFF`) and then stops polling - otherwise a dead poll costs every open packs tab a 404 every 20s forever.

`GET /api/admin/goat-poll/voters?nomineeId=` is the ballot behind one row: who voted, which way, and when. Deliberately not on the public board - a poll that names your downvotes is a poll people vote on differently - but the person who has to decide what the result meant cannot otherwise tell `+8` built out of one player's friends from `+8` the community agreed on. Voter names come from the `users` projection, then from `analytics_viewers` (`AnalyticsStore.getViewerNames`, the other database, best effort) for anyone off the tracked rosters, then not at all: a bare id is still a voter, and no osu! API call is spent on this. The frontend side is `fetchGoatPollVoters` behind `requireTrueAdminAccess`, and the widget's row is only clickable when `auth.isAdmin` - the same narrower flag that gates removal, not the wider dev-access one that decides who can *see* an unreleased poll.
